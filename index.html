💡 Why This Split Works For You
🦵
Wednesday's leg day directly trains the muscles that protect your meniscus + ACL (quads, hamstrings, hip abductors), plus balance work — proprioception training is one of the most evidence-backed pieces of ACL rehab, and it's now built in
⏱️
Every gym day is time-boxed to fit your 1-hour office slot: warm-up + workout + cooldown
🏃
Running progresses via run-walk intervals — a standard graduated return-to-running protocol, same idea physios use after this kind of injury
📆
Leg day (Wed) sits 2 days before your next run (Fri), giving your knee time to recover before load-bearing running
😴
Mon + Sat rest days don't break your streak — they're built into the plan, not missed days
✅ During Any Session
💪
Muscle burn = okay, keep going
🛑
Joint pain = stop immediately, don't push through
⚠️
Swelling or nyut-nyutan the next morning = you did too much, back off next session
❌ Still Avoid For Now
🚫
Padel, badminton, or anything with sudden pivots/lateral cuts
🚫
Deep squats (below 45°) or deep lunges
🚫
Hiking downhill / uneven terrain
🚫
Skipping warm-up before running — cold joints jumping into jogging is asking for re-injury
🚨 See Doctor / Physio Sooner If
🔴
Knee locks and won't straighten
🔴
Sudden swelling returns
🔴
Knee gives way during normal walking
🔴
Pain returns even at rest, not just after activity
📋 Ongoing Care
🏥
Keep your monthly physio visits going even as running resumes
❄️
Ice 15–20 min after any session that leaves the knee feeling warm or achy
💊
Welmove + Omega-3 daily, taken with meals
Backup: Gentle exercises for a flare-up day
Quad Sets — 3×15, hold 10s. Sit with leg straight, push knee into a rolled towel.
Straight Leg Raises — 3×12, hold 5s. Lie flat, lift straight leg to 45°.
Glute Bridges — 3×12, hold 5s. Lie on back, lift hips, squeeze glutes.
Prone Heel Slides — 3×10. Lie on stomach, bend knee, heel toward glutes.
Swap today's planned session for these 4 if the knee feels off — resume normal plan once it settles.
